# Revision 0.2 — ground, motion and utility interfaces

Date: September 7, 2026. Status: pre-feasibility concept.

## Audit finding

The prior repository animated alternating rings at 0.5 m/s and sketched an inner-edge multi-passage rotary union. It did not establish a ground load path, support/rail arrangement, structural articulation, drive/braking zones, foundation concept, equipment replacement method or credible full-scale water/sewage interface. The rooftop-waterfall and giant-union ideas are therefore superseded.

## Selected hypotheses for professional study

| System | Revision 0.2 hypothesis | Status |
|---|---|---|
| Moving structure | ~200 m articulated structural modules | Scope assumption |
| Vertical support | Redundant bogies on 3 circumferential rail bands | Scope assumption |
| Lateral/uplift | Separate guide rollers and hold-down devices | Functional requirement |
| Drive | Distributed electric traction zones | Options study required |
| Braking | Independent service/emergency braking zones | Safety analysis required |
| Ground | Stationary guideway on site-derived continuous/piled foundations | Site required |
| Potable water | Fixed loops + synchronized sanitary transfer carriages + ring buffers | Selected to test |
| Wastewater | Sector sumps/tanks + separate solids-rated transfer carriages | Selected to test |
| First rig | Stop, interlock, connect, transfer, isolate, release | Selected demonstrator |

The calculation source produces about 601 modules and 14,340 bogie positions from the current spacing assumptions. These expose the scale of the machinery; they do not specify equipment. Candidate 120-second controlled and 30-second emergency stops correspond to idealized 30 m and 7.5 m stopping distances from 0.5 m/s. Force, torque, heat and foundations require a mass and resistance model.

## Freshwater decision

The open top-fed scheme is rejected for contamination exposure and lifting all flow to a roof header even when it is consumed lower down. Ground-fed supply still needs elevation head for upper floors; it does not eliminate that energy requirement. Hub-fed water is rejected because outer supply crosses multiple inner moving boundaries. Ground-fed loops create one controlled boundary per ring and direct maintenance access.

Six water transfer bays per ring, one unavailable, and 12 hours of equalization storage are study assumptions. At the current demand model, citywide water is about 777 ML/day and the equalization volume is about 389 ML. The largest preliminary transfer-bay duty is about 1.12 m³/s while connected. Fire water, cooling, irrigation, freeboard and emergency reserve are excluded.

## Wastewater decision

The preferred full-city research hypothesis uses separate traveling carriages: match ring speed, verify alignment, couple, pump, isolate, drain the coupling cavity, disconnect and reset. Eight bays per ring and 50% connection duty are study assumptions. The largest preliminary bay is about 1.14 m³/s. A stopped docking rig remains the first experiment because it provides the most controlled way to test solids, leakage, surge, seals and failures.

## Release conditions

Do not advance to schematic design until a multidisciplinary team completes the mass/load model, geotechnical basis, structural articulation and fatigue study, drive/brake hazard analysis, public transfer and evacuation strategy, potable-water quality/hydraulic model, wastewater fault model, full-size endurance testing and whole-life cost comparison against static alternatives.
